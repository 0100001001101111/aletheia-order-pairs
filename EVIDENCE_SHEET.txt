Reason: Q contains a K3,3 subdivision with branches {1,2,7} and {3,8,9}: paths 1-3, 1-8, 1-9, 2-3, 2-8, 2-9,
7-3, 7-6-8, 7-4-9. Its vertex-edge order is not a circle order by Scheinerman's theorem. A finite strict 2+1
embedding would yield disk containment (slightly contract times to remove incomparable null pairs, then add
a common positive radius). Hence Q cannot embed. The direct integer certificate proves P embeds. The
cospectral regular graphs are van Dam and Haemers (2003), Figure 2; this graph pair is prior work.

Reason: put Q(t,x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2-t^2 and eta=diag(-1,1,1,1). Lift the seven bottoms to (-2 eta x_i,Q(x_i))
in R^5. Radon gives equal disjoint convex combinations. One support S has at most three points. A has a top
T above exactly S. Q(T-x_i) is affine in the lift; its weighted value is negative on S and nonnegative on
the other support, a contradiction. For B the rational coordinates verify every ordered pair. By top size
the two incidence row Grams are I, 5I+J, 10I+5J. Exact block identities give equality on both lists. This
does not exclude higher-dimensional embeddings of A.
