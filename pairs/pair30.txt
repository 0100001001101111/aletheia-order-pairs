30-element pair: P embeds strictly in 2+1; Q does not.

Points 0..9 are graph vertices. For each edge in the JSON's P_edges or Q_edges list, point 10+k is its edge point, where k starts at 0. The only covers are u<10+k and v<10+k for edge(u,v). The explicit cover lists are also in the JSON. Coordinates for P are ordered by point index and each row is(t,x,y). All coordinates are integers.

The graphs are the cospectral planar/nonplanar pair of van Dam and Haemers(2003), Figure 2. Both are simple 4-regular graphs with 10 vertices and 20 edges. Their incidence row Gram matrix is 4I+A. This fixes the relevant spectra and the degree data and gives equality on both fixed lists.

Why Q cannot embed: its graph contains the K 3,3 subdivision with branches{1,2,7} and{3,8,9} and paths 1-3,1-8,1-9,2-3,2-8,2-9,7-3,7-6-8,7-4-9. The internal vertices are disjoint. Scheinerman's theorem says the vertex-edge order of a finite simple graph is a circle order exactly when that graph is planar. A finite strict 2+1 embedding would yield such disks: shrink all times by a common factor sufficiently close to 1 so timelike relations remain and incomparable null pairs become spacelike, then add a common constant so all times are positive and use times as disk radii. This contradicts nonplanarity. The positive coordinate certificate is checked directly, not inferred from planarity.

These are two-layer finite orders. No faithful sampling or physical-spacetime claim is made. The paper's perturbation method was not tested.
